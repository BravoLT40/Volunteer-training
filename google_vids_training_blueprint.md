# 🎬 Google Vids Production Blueprint: The Pi Academy Volunteer Training

This production guide is structured specifically for **Google Vids** (`vids.google.com`). You can use this document in two ways:
1. **AI Prompt to Video**: Copy the **Master Prompt** below into Google Vids' "Help me create" / prompt box to generate an automated draft.
2. **Scene-by-Scene Studio Build**: Follow the detailed storyboard table and narration scripts below to drop text, assets, voiceovers, and transitions directly into Google Vids scenes.

---

## ⚡ Method 1: Google Vids "Help Me Create" Master Prompt

Copy and paste this into the Google Vids prompt box:

> **Google Vids Prompt:**
> "Create a 5 to 6 minute vibrant, energetic volunteer orientation and training video for The Pi Academy's Florida Youth Coding Camp. The audience is new camp volunteers mentoring middle school students (ages 10-13). 
> 
> Style: Clean, modern, warm, tech-educational with raspberry red, emerald green, and tech blue accents. Upbeat, inspiring background music.
> 
> Video structure:
> 1. Introduction: Mission of The Pi Academy (thepiacademy.org), empowering youth, and the Golden Rule: 'Never touch the camper's keyboard or mouse.'
> 2. Camp Day Overview: Walkthrough of the 5-hour camp timeline (Hardware setup, Scratch, Lunch, Python/Robotics, Hands-on Stations, and Project Showcase).
> 3. Station 1: Drone Station (Tello EDU, DroneBlocks 4-block mission, iPad Guided Access, flight safety boundaries).
> 4. Station 2: Pizza Delivery Game (Pygame, medieval platformer, Developer Mode 'M' key, modding Level 2 & 3, git reset troubleshooting).
> 5. Station 3: Simple Website (Single-file HTML/CSS/JS, inspect element tool, camper survey mod).
> 6. Station 4: Turtle Race (Python turtle graphics, code quality, comparing hardcoded scripts to dynamic list-based code).
> 7. Station 5 & 6: GoPiGo Robotics (Obstacle course navigation and Pi Photo camera challenges).
> 8. Conclusion: Day 1 volunteer readiness checklist, encouragement, and thank you."

---

## 🎞️ Method 2: Detailed Scene-by-Scene Storyboard & Voiceover Scripts

Use these exact scenes inside the Google Vids editor.

---

### Scene 1: Welcome & Mission
* **Scene Title**: Welcome to The Pi Academy
* **Duration**: ~35 seconds
* **Visuals & Layout**:
  * Title Card with Pi Academy Logo (`π`) and headline: *"Florida Youth Coding Camp: Volunteer Training"*.
  * Stock video / B-roll: Energetic middle schoolers smiling, collaborating around computers and electronics.
  * Lower-Third Banner: *The Pi Academy • www.thepiacademy.org*
* **On-Screen Text**:
  * *Empowering Middle Schoolers Through Code*
  * *From Consumers to Creators*
* **AI Voiceover Script (Word-for-Word)**:
  > "Welcome to The Pi Academy team! Thank you for stepping up to mentor middle schoolers at our Florida Youth Coding Camp. At The Pi Academy, our mission is simple but powerful: we introduce 10 to 13-year-olds to the superpower of computer science, hardware, and robotics. For many campers, this is their very first time seeing code or touching a Raspberry Pi. Your energy, patience, and encouragement will make an impact that lasts well beyond this week."

---

### Scene 2: The Golden Rule of Mentoring
* **Scene Title**: The Golden Rule
* **Duration**: ~30 seconds
* **Visuals & Layout**:
  * Split screen or bold card with warning/highlight banner: *"The Golden Rule of Volunteering"*.
  * Graphic: Hands pointing toward a monitor screen (NOT touching the keyboard).
* **On-Screen Text**:
  * *Rule #1: Never touch the camper's mouse or keyboard!*
  * *Guide with questions. Let THEM fix the bugs.*
* **AI Voiceover Script**:
  > "Before we dive into the stations, here is our number one golden rule: Never touch a camper's keyboard or mouse. When code breaks, don't fix it for them. Instead, point to the screen, ask them to read the error message aloud, and guide their eyes to the line number. When students type the fix themselves and see their program come to life, that’s where true confidence is born."

---

### Scene 3: The Camp Day Timeline
* **Scene Title**: Camp Day at a Glance
* **Duration**: ~40 seconds
* **Visuals & Layout**:
  * Animated timeline graphic or step-by-step numbered cards showing the 5-hour flow.
  * Clock icon transitions.
* **On-Screen Text**:
  * *Phase 1 (Hours 1–2): Setting Up the Pi Computers*
  * *Unboxing, Hardware Assembly, MicroSD & Peripherals*
  * *First Boot & Raspberry Pi OS Desktop Orientation*
  * *Lunch: Pizza Time! 🍕*
  * *Phase 2 (Hours 3–5): Rotating Through the Stations*
  * *Round 1 & 2: Drones, GoPiGo Robotics, Game Dev, Web/Scratch*
  * *Hour 5: Project Showcase & Camper Demos*
* **AI Voiceover Script**:
  > "Here is how our 5-hour camp day flows. The first two hours are dedicated to setting up the Raspberry Pi computers—from unboxing components and connecting displays to booting up into the desktop. After a pizza lunch break, campers spend the next three hours rotating through our hands-on interactive stations: drones, robotics, game programming, and web design. Finally, the day wraps up with our camper showcase where students demo their creations for friends and parents!"

---

### Scene 4: Drone Station (Tello EDU & DroneBlocks)
* **Scene Title**: Station 1 • Drone Station
* **Duration**: ~45 seconds
* **Visuals & Layout**:
  * Video of a mini drone taking off from one table and landing on another.
  * Graphic of the 4 DroneBlocks coding blocks: `Takeoff` → `Fly Up 20 in` → `Fly Forward [X] in` → `Land`.
  * Caution Badge: *Safety First • Spectator Boundary*.
* **On-Screen Text**:
  * *Hardware: DJI Tello EDU + iPad*
  * *App: DroneBlocks with Guided Access*
  * *Roles: Measurer, Programmer, Observer*
  * *Safety: Never touch or catch a flying drone!*
* **AI Voiceover Script**:
  > "At the Drone Station, pairs of campers program a Tello EDU drone to fly autonomously from Table A to Table B. Students assign roles: Measurer, Programmer, and Observer. They measure the exact distance in inches and build a four-block program in DroneBlocks. 
  > 
  > Volunteers: make sure the iPad is locked in Guided Access before handing it to students. Enforce our spectator boundary, and remember: students must never reach for or catch a flying drone. Always wait until the propellers come to a complete stop."

---

### Scene 5: Pizza Delivery Game (Pygame Modding)
* **Scene Title**: Station 2 • Pizza Delivery Game
* **Duration**: ~45 seconds
* **Visuals & Layout**:
  * Gameplay clip or screenshot of a 2D retro pixel-art pizza platformer with guards and platforms.
  * Callout box highlighting the `M` key for Developer Grid.
* **On-Screen Text**:
  * *Stack: Python 3.14 & Pygame*
  * *Challenge: Levels 2 & 3 require code editing!*
  * *Press 'M': Developer Grid Overlay (X, Y)*
  * *Rescue Command: git reset --hard*
* **AI Voiceover Script**:
  > "Next up: the Pizza Delivery Game! Students play a medieval delivery hero wielding a breadstick sword and a pizza cannon. But here’s the twist: levels two and three are intentionally impossible to beat without opening the code and modifying Python files! 
  > 
  > Teach campers to press the 'M' key to toggle Developer Mode and see tile coordinates. Guide them to delete obstacle walls in Level2.py and add platform bridges across pits. If they introduce syntax errors and break the game, don’t panic—just run 'git reset --hard' in the terminal to restore the code instantly."

---

### Scene 6: Simple Website & Turtle Race
* **Scene Title**: Stations 3 & 4 • Web Dev & Turtle Race
* **Duration**: ~40 seconds
* **Visuals & Layout**:
  * Split screen: Left shows HTML/CSS code next to a browser preview; Right shows colorful Python turtle racers sprinting across track lines.
* **On-Screen Text**:
  * *Simple Website: HTML (Skeleton) • CSS (Clothing) • JS (Brains)*
  * *Pro Tip: Teach Right-Click → Inspect Element!*
  * *Turtle Race: Code Quality • Lists & Functions*
* **AI Voiceover Script**:
  > "At the Simple Website station, campers explore a single-file webpage. They discover how HTML provides the skeleton, CSS adds styling, and JavaScript handles button clicks. Show them how to right-click and 'Inspect' the page!
  > 
  > At the Turtle Race station, students learn the difference between messy copy-pasted code and professional code architecture. By adding their favorite colors to a Python list, the program automatically spawns new racers and redraws the numbered track."

---

### Scene 7: GoPiGo Robotics & Camera Stations
* **Scene Title**: Stations 5 & 6 • GoPiGo Robotics
* **Duration**: ~35 seconds
* **Visuals & Layout**:
  * Video/photo of a two-wheeled Raspberry Pi GoPiGo rover on the floor and a close-up of the Pi Camera module.

  * *GoPiGo Obstacle Course: Motor control & distance sensors*

At the Pi Photo station, campers use Python to control the mounted Pi Camera, capturing snapshots and experimenting with digital filters. Keep an eye on battery connections and floor boundaries so rovers have plenty of room to roam."

## 🛠️ Step-by-Step Instructions: How to Build It in Google Vids

1. **Navigate to Google Vids**: Go to [vids.google.com](https://vids.google.com) (available through Google Workspace).
2. **Start a New Video**:
   * Click **"Help me create a video"**.
   * Paste the **Master Prompt** from Method 1 above. Google Vids will automatically generate a storyboard draft with suggested scenes, layouts, and stock media.
3. **Refine with the Scene-by-Scene Scripts**:
   * Click on each scene in the Google Vids timeline.
   * Open the **Voiceover / Audio track** and select **Text-to-Speech (AI Voiceover)**. Choose a clear, energetic voice (e.g., *Natural Warm* or *Professional Bright*).
   * Copy-paste the exact **AI Voiceover Script** provided for each scene above.
   * Adjust the scene duration to match the spoken audio.
4. **Brand Assets & Theme**:
   * Set brand accent colors: **Pi Red (`#C51A4A`)**, **Tech Blue (`#2684FF`)**, and **Emerald Green (`#00875A`)**.
   * Upload The Pi Academy logo for the intro and outro scenes.
5. **Publish & Share**:
   * Click **Share** (top right) to generate a viewable link or export as an MP4 video file to email or display to incoming volunteers!
